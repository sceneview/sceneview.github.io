CameraPathPlayer

@Stable
class CameraPathPlayer(timeline: CameraTimeline, loop: Boolean = timeline.loop)

Plays a CameraTimeline: a playhead with play, pause, seek and loop.

The player owns the playhead, seconds, and the number of whole laps a looping player has played. The pose comes from the timeline at that time: on the first lap it is CameraTimeline.poseAt at seconds, so playback, scrubbing and an offline render at a fixed step agree on the pose for a given time. On a later lap of a path whose turns are not whole (a CameraPath.tour or CameraPosePath.orbit of 1.5 turns), pose adds the turn of the laps played, so it no longer equals timeline.poseAt(seconds): lap two of 1.5 turns is half a turn on. restart goes back to the first lap. advance moves the playhead by the frame's time, not by a fixed step per frame: a dropped frame does not slow the move down, and a 30 fps and a 120 fps device reach the same pose at the same time.

Its state is Compose snapshot state, so a slider or a clock that reads seconds or isPlaying recomposes as the move plays. Call advance or advanceFrame from the main thread, typically from SceneView's onFrame, and write the returned pose with io.github.sceneview.node.CameraNode.applyPose:

val player = rememberCameraPathPlayer(path)
SceneView(
    cameraNode = cameraNode,
    cameraManipulator = null, // the path owns the camera
    frameRatePolicy = FrameRatePolicy.Continuous(),
    onFrame = { nanos -> cameraNode.applyPose(player.advanceFrame(nanos)) },
)

The step is not capped: after a long stall the playhead jumps to where the wall clock says it should be. CameraAnimator caps its step instead, because it simulates a spring; a timeline has no state to destabilize.

Remember the timeline. Build it inside remember(...) keyed on its inputs, not in the composable body: a timeline built on every composition is a new object every time, which rebuilds the spline on every recomposition. The player does not recompose because of it (only a changed durationSeconds or loop is observable), but the work is wasted.

Offline rendering. The player follows the wall clock. To render a video at a fixed rate, sample the timeline directly, timeline.poseAt(frameIndex / fps), rather than stepping a player.

Parameters

timeline

The move to play.

loop

Whether the playhead wraps at the end. Defaults to the timeline's own CameraTimeline.loop. Forcing it on a timeline built with loop = false carries the turn of a fractional CameraPosePath.orbit or CameraPath.tour across the seam, but such a timeline keeps its non-looping ends: the speed can change at the seam, and a tour of 1.5 turns ends at the top of its rise, so its elevation jumps there. Build the timeline with loop = true for a seamless loop.

pose
Link copied to clipboard
val pose: CameraPose

The pose at the playhead: timeline.poseAt(seconds), plus the turn of the laps played for a CameraPath or CameraPosePath.orbit whose turns are not whole, when the player loops (its own loop, not only the timeline's). A CameraPath.tour of 1.5 turns starts its second lap half a turn on, with no jump at the seam, and stays exact after days of play: the player keeps a whole lap count and the time within the lap, and the path reduces the laps' turn to under one turn in double precision before it reaches a float. Another CameraTimeline is sampled within the lap, so it should end where it starts to loop seamlessly. seconds and progress stay within the lap.

timeline
Link copied to clipboard
var timeline: CameraTimeline

The move being played. Replacing it keeps the playhead time and brings it into the new duration at once, wrapped when loop is set and clamped otherwise: a looping player at 15 s that switches from a 20 s to a 10 s timeline goes on from 5 s, and seconds, progress and pose agree from the next read. The lap count is kept too, so a rebuilt CameraPath.tour of 1.5 turns does not jump back half a turn. A new timeline with a different turn per lap applies its own turn to the laps already played, so the camera can jump when the turn count changes mid-run; call restart to start the new move from its first lap.

fun advance(deltaSeconds: Float): CameraPose

Moves the playhead by deltaSeconds of wall-clock time times speed while playing, and returns the pose at the new playhead. A non-looping player stops when the playhead reaches the end it is heading for: the end playing forward, the start playing backward. At speed 0 it keeps playing in place.

restart

fun restart()

Plays from the start of the first lap: the lap count goes back to 0, the playhead to 0, and playback starts. The pose is then timeline.poseAt(0), the timeline's own start, even after laps of a CameraPath.tour of 1.5 turns. Use it for a "replay" button; seekTo stays within the current lap.

seekTo
Link copied to clipboard
fun seekTo(seconds: Float)

Moves the playhead to seconds within the current lap. [0, durationSeconds] is taken as is, both ends included, so seeking to durationSeconds shows the end of the lap (progress 1) on every lap. Outside it, the time is wrapped when loop is set and clamped otherwise, still within the lap. The lap count is kept, so on lap two of a CameraPath.tour of 1.5 turns seekTo(0f) shows the start of lap two, half a turn on from the timeline's start; restart goes back to the first lap. Playback state is unchanged. A non-finite value is ignored.
